Tools a Cowboy Would Use launches dawnanddusk.earth

A web companion to the iPhone app, where anyone can search a place on a map and see when the sun rises and sets, with a public gallery of other people's mornings and evenings.

By Lucas Pfeiffer and Claude

October 8, 20261 min read

In January 2026, Dawn and Dusk got an address on the open web. The site at dawnanddusk.earth is a small map application: type a place, or let the browser find you, and it shows sunrise and sunset for that spot, with a table for the days ahead and a toggle between a flat map and a globe. It defaults to San Francisco, where the app was made.

No announcement marked the launch. The site's first certificates were issued on January 15, 2026, and its maker's personal site linked to it the same day, which is as close to a birthday as the record gives.

Why a website

The iPhone app is a one-time purchase with no accounts. The website asks nothing at all. It lets someone who has never heard of the app see what it sees, and it gives the app's community gallery a public home: the gallery page on dawnanddusk.earth shows the sunrises and sunsets that app users choose to share. When the gallery was empty it said so with some grace: "Nothing here yet. The next sunrise is somebody's first photo."

The site is static, hosted on GitHub Pages, and built on OpenStreetMap data. Like everything the brand makes, it is designed to be private and to give time back rather than take it.
