Tools a Cowboy Would Use launches Dawn and Dusk

An iPhone app for sunrise and sunset, with two notifications a day and nothing collected, became the first product of an art project that makes apps the way a clothing brand makes accessories.

By Lucas Pfeiffer and Claude

October 8, 20261 min read

On February 24, 2025, version 1.0 of Dawn and Dusk appeared on the iOS App Store. It does one thing: it knows when the sun will rise and set where you are, and it tells you shortly before each. The listing's subtitle is "Sync your soul with the sun." A line from its early description has stuck to it since: "like a prayer bell for the sun."

The app was the first product of Tools a Cowboy Would Use, an art project from San Francisco that describes itself as a clothing brand that makes consumer apps as accessories. The brand's website went up a few weeks after the app, in March 2025, with the tagline "making tools people love." The app's reasoning is stated plainly on that site: our biology is tied to the sun, and experiencing its transitions helps unlock more of what we are.

What it did

At launch, Dawn and Dusk computed sunrise and sunset from the phone's location, drew a countdown to the next one, and sent two notifications a day. Over the first year it grew in small steps. Sunrise and sunset could be added to the calendar by April 2025. Weather arrived the same month so the app could "tell time with the sun." By August it measured time spent in daylight and tracked vitamin D as a beta. In September it took on the new iOS 26 look.

Three things have not changed. It is a one-time purchase, with no subscription. It has no accounts and no ads. And it collects nothing: location stays on the device, weather comes from Apple's WeatherKit, and the privacy policy says the app "does not collect, store, or share any personal information."

Reception

The App Store listing has held a five-star average on a small number of ratings. The app has no press to speak of. Its audience grew the way the brand intended, by hand, and its first-year reviews were quoted back on the product page: "The only app I use in the mornings."
